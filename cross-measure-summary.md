## 2. Conflicting / countervailing measures on the same ballot

### The Props 40 / 41 / 42 tax cluster — the biggest money story
These three measures are a single, deliberately linked fight:

- **Prop 40** (one-time "billionaire" wealth tax) is **supported** by SEIU-UHW (~$31.5M) and **opposed**
  by Building a Better California (~$88.5M of a $99.6M oppose side).
- **Props 41 and 42** (audit requirement; ban on new personal-property/retroactive taxes) are
  **supported** by Building a Better California ($58.35M and $97.8M) and formally **opposed** by the
  SEIU-UHW "Yes on 40" committee.

So the same two camps face each other three times. **Avoiding double counting:** Building a Better
California's money appears as ~$88.5M (oppose 40) + $58.35M (support 41) + $97.8M (support 42) — these
are *different checks to different committees*, legitimately distinct. But the **SEIU-UHW ~$31.5M is a
single pot**: it is the Prop 40 support money, and although FPPC cross-lists it as "No on 41 & 42," the
SOS does **not** add it to the Prop 41/42 oppose totals (which are only $703,494 each). Do not treat the
~$31.5M as additional opposition spending on 41 and 42.

### Prop 3 vs. Props 40/41/42 (revenue direction)
Prop 3 (extend high-income tax for schools/health) and Prop 40 (new wealth tax) both raise taxes on
high earners and share the "Tax the Ultra-Rich Now" committee ($703,494) on their support side, which
simultaneously opposes the anti-tax Props 41 and 42.

### Within-side transfers (not conflicts, but double-count risks)
- **Prop 39 opposition:** "No on 39 – Block Election Rigging" transferred $2,000,000 to "United Against
  Prop. 39"; counted in both committees within the $29.6M oppose total.
- **Prop 45 opposition:** "No on Prop 45 (labor/health/env)" transferred $1,500,000 to "Campaign for
  People over Polluters"; counted in both within the $18.9M oppose total.
- **Prop 1 support:** a $700,000 transfer between the two Yes-on-1 committees.

## 3. Ballot-argument signatories whose campaign money is small vs. the main funders

Based on the FPPC top-10 lists, several organizations that lend their *names* to campaigns are minor
*funders* relative to the dominant donor:

- **Prop 40 opposition** is branded "Doctors, Teachers, Small Businesses, Working Families and Public
  Safety," and the California Medical Association and California Primary Care Association Advocates each
  gave **$100,000** — but **99.75%** of that committee ($88.5M) is Building a Better California.
- **Props 41 / 42 support** are branded as coalitions of "small businesses, taxpayers, retirees,
  blue-collar workers, seniors, veterans" — but are ~99.96% / ~88% a single tech-donor committee.
- **Prop 44 opposition** names the California Primary Care Association, which gave ~9.6%; the clinics
  (AltaMed etc.) provide the bulk.
- **Prop 45 opposition** is branded "labor, health and environmental organizations," but NRDC Action
  Fund ($25,000), Earthjustice ($209k) and others are small next to Clean and Healthy California
  ($7.08M) and Wendy Schmidt ($5M).

# California November 3, 2026 Ballot Measure Funding Report

**Data date (FPPC "Last Modified"): October 6, 2026.** All figures in this report reflect
campaign-finance filings reported through that date. The FPPC page updates as new filings
arrive, so later filings are not reflected here.

This report covers the 14 statewide propositions on California's November 3, 2026 general
election ballot: **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45.** This list was
confirmed against the Secretary of State's Official Voter Information Guide
(https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/) and the SOS ballot-measure contribution-totals
index on October 8, 2026.

Each proposition has its own file: `prop-01.md` through `prop-45.md` (only the numbers that
appear on the ballot). A cross-measure analysis is in `cross-measure-summary.md`.

---

## What this report is

The goal is to let a voter see **who is actually paying** for each campaign, because committee
names (for example, "No on Prop 40 – Doctors, Teachers, Small Businesses, Working Families and
Public Safety...") often reveal nothing about the donors. Where money passes through an
intermediary committee, this report traces it back toward the original donors as far as the
public record allows.

## Sources

1. **FPPC Top 10 Contributors, November 2026 General Election** (primary source for each
   committee's top donors and "Top Donors to Contributor" notes):
   https://www.fppc.ca.gov/search-filings/top-10-contributors-list/november-2026-general-election/
2. **Secretary of State ballot-measure contribution totals** (per-measure total support and
   oppose dollars, including committees below the FPPC threshold):
   https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/helpful-resources/measure-contributions/2026-ballot-measure-contribution-totals
3. **Official Voter Information Guide** (official titles):
   https://voterguide.sos.ca.gov/propositions/
4. **News reporting** (used only to attach dollar amounts to donors named-but-not-quantified on
   the official lists, and to identify who a donor is). Every news-sourced figure is labeled
   with its outlet and date.

## Known limits — read before using the numbers

- **FPPC thresholds.** The FPPC "Top 10" lists cover only committees that raised at least
  **$1,000,000**, and within those committees only contributors of **$10,000 or more**.
  Smaller committees and smaller donors are not shown on the FPPC list. (The SOS per-measure
  totals *do* capture smaller committees, so SOS totals are frequently larger than the sum of
  the FPPC top-10 lines.)
- **Asterisk / double-counting.** A contributor marked with an asterisk (`*`) on the FPPC page
  gave to a committee that supports or opposes **more than one** measure, so the same dollars
  appear under each of those measures. **These figures are not added across measures**, and the
  flag is repeated wherever it applies. The clearest example is the "Yes on 40 – Billionaire Tax
  Now" committee, whose contributions appear under Prop 40 (support) **and** under Props 41 and
  42 (oppose) — the same money, shown three times.
- **"Top Donors to Contributor" notes show names, not amounts.** Where the FPPC annotates an
  intermediary committee with the names of its own donors, those notes do **not** give a dollar
  split. This report does not invent a split. Where an amount is needed it is taken from news
  coverage and labeled as news-sourced.
- **Cal-Access.** Cal-Access (cal-access.sos.ca.gov), the state's itemized filing database,
  disallows automated access, so itemized filings were **not** scraped. Each proposition file
  includes the Cal-Access committee links so you can verify the detail manually.
- **No qualifying committee.** Where no committee on a side reached the FPPC threshold, the file
  says so plainly rather than leaving a blank.

## Method (one paragraph)

For each proposition this report records the official ballot title, the FPPC and SOS total
support/oppose dollars (flagging any discrepancy between the two rather than silently
reconciling them), and for each committee its top contributors with amounts. Where a contributor
is itself a committee, the "Top Donors to Contributor" note is followed one level (and further
where sources allow) toward the original donors, keeping a clear line between **filed fact**
(FPPC/SOS), **news-sourced** (outlet + date), and **unverified**. Each top funder's share of its
committee's total is shown with the arithmetic. Donors are described only as the sources describe
them; where general knowledge is used it is labeled; motives are not characterized, though a
short clearly-labeled "interests to be aware of" note is included where it follows directly from
the sources.

## Things that could not be verified — check these manually

- **Prop 39 support total vs. Uihlein's $17M.** The FPPC lists Richard Uihlein at $17,000,000 to
  "Californians for Voter ID, Yes on 39," but the SOS reports that committee's total support at
  **$15,785,413** — i.e., the single donor exceeds the committee total. The $17M appears to be a
  cumulative *campaign* figure (spanning the "Reform California" qualification committee and the
  Yes committee) rather than a single committee receipt. Verify on Cal-Access. See `prop-39.md`.
- **Itemized amounts behind intermediary committees** (Building a Better California, Homeownership
  for Families, Reform California, Protect Prop. 13, California Business Roundtable Issues PAC,
  Clean and Healthy California, etc.) — the per-donor splits into these committees are not on the
  FPPC/SOS pages used here; news-sourced amounts are labeled as such and should be confirmed
  against Cal-Access.
- Any committee that raised less than $1M and reported no detail to SOS.

## Summary of findings

**Sources used:** FPPC Top 10 Contributors (primary, "Last Modified: October 6, 2026"), the 14 SOS
per-proposition contribution-total pages, the Official Voter Information Guide for titles, and targeted
news searches only to attach amounts/identities to donors the official lists name but don't quantify
(labeled with outlet + date). Cal-Access was **not** scraped — each file links to it for manual
verification.

**Proposition list confirmed:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45.

**Key findings:**
- **Building a Better California** (Brin/Doerr/Khosla tech money) is the ballot's most far-reaching
  funder — near-sole backer of Yes-on-41 (~100%), Yes-on-42 (~88%), No-on-40 (99.75%), plus Props 37,
  45, and 1.
- **Props 40/41/42 are one linked fight:** SEIU-UHW vs. Building a Better California, facing off three
  times.
- **Committee names routinely hide the donor** — e.g. "No on Prop 40 – Doctors, Teachers, Small
  Businesses..." is 99.75% one tech committee; the "coalition of retirees/veterans" Yes-on-42 is ~88%
  the same source.

**Flagged rather than silently resolved (see per-file notes):**
1. **Prop 39:** FPPC lists Uihlein at $17M, which exceeds the SOS committee total ($15.79M) — it appears
   to be a cross-committee campaign figure. News-named donors (Gebbia $8M, Bray >$3M) aren't in the FPPC
   top-10.
2. **FPPC-vs-SOS magnitude mismatches** on Props 3, 37, 43, 44 (FPPC top-10 lines sum to more than the
   SOS committee total — cumulative vs. reported receipts). Amounts shown "as filed," not summed.
3. **Prop 45:** a support committee (BRIDGE/PG&E $2.4M) and an oppose committee (#1479255) appear on
   FPPC but not as separate SOS lines.
4. **Double-counting handled explicitly:** asterisked shared committees ("Tax the Ultra-Rich Now,"
   "Communities for an Affordable California," SEIU-UHW's Prop 40 money cross-listed as 41/42 opposition)
   and intra-side transfers (Props 39, 45, 1) are all flagged and never added across measures.

Props 2 and 5 have no reported committees on either side; Prop 4 has only one small support committee
below the FPPC $1M threshold.
